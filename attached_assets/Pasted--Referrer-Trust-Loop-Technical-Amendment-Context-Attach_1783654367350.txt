# Referrer Trust Loop Technical Amendment

## Context

Attachment `#140 - Referrer Trust Loop` describes a broken trust loop in the
current OKU/Foodie application:
Referrers drive reservations through QR codes and links, but the current OKU
app does not give them a reliable live view of what they referred or a
trustworthy view of the money their referrals generated.

- Streetside referrers can create reservations through QR/link flows.
- The referrer-facing active feed can still show `No active bookings`.
- The QR scanner may be an anonymous guest using a mobile phone; the feed owner
  is the street host account that owns the scanned QR/link.
- The feed appears to use a fixed short look-back window and narrow gating.
- Future-dated referrals can be hidden even when the reservation was created
  successfully.
- The backend trust chain can capture check close, commission, ledger, and
  payout state, but the referrer does not see that chain.
The confirmed streetside bug:

The referenced implementation files are not present in this local workspace, so
this amendment is written as a patch-ready technical spec for the app codebase.
- An anonymous guest scans a street host QR.
- The reservation is created successfully.
- The restaurant host dashboard sees the reservation.
- The street host account that owns the QR still sees `No active bookings`.

## Red-Team Findings
That narrows the failure to the referrer-facing feed and/or ownership
resolution, not reservation creation itself.

### 1. The 8-hour look-back creates false negatives
## Verified Red-Team Findings

A referrer feed must not be based on reservation creation time or a short
look-back window. Referral usefulness is based on service time and referral
ownership, not recent creation.
### 1. Feed Window Is The Bug

Risk:
`mySubmissions` filters by `createdAt >= now - 8h`, and this is the only feed a
pure streetside host gets. `todayReservations` is gated to restaurant host or
superadmin.

- A future reservation created today for tomorrow disappears immediately.
- Referrers lose trust because successful QR reservations look missing.
- Rollup totals undercount active contribution.
Failure mode:

- A referral can show for about eight hours after creation.
- It then disappears regardless of the actual reservation service date.
- Future-dated referrals are especially vulnerable.

Amendment:

- Active referrals must be selected by reservation service window and referrer
  ownership.
- History referrals must be selected by terminal status or expired service
  window.
- The viewer does not need to be the guest/scanner. The viewer must be the
  referrer actor that owns the QR/link used to create the reservation.
- Select active referrals by reservation service window and referrer ownership.
- Do not use reservation creation time as the active-feed boundary.
- Use Panama-local service windows for launch behavior.

### 2. Referrer surfaces can drift
### 2. Referrer Surfaces Already Drift

Separate streetside, referrer, influencer, and partner dashboards invite
different query rules.
Streetside reads `AttributionSession.referralActor`.

Risk:
The referrer dashboard reads legacy `ReservationAttribution` rows plus
`CommissionEntry`.

- Streetside sees nothing while influencer sees data.
- Financial fields appear in one place and not another.
- Fixes are applied to the broken tab only, leaving the platform inconsistent.
Actor-only chains such as host links and `INFLUENCER_SUB_REFERRER` may have no
`ReservationAttribution` row, so they are invisible on the referrer dashboard.

Amendment:

- Build one shared server data source for `myReferrals`.
- Consume that source from every referrer-facing surface.
- Keep SSR and polling/API refresh on the same include/projection.
- Build the shared source from `AttributionSession`, because it is the superset.
- Stop treating `ReservationAttribution` as the canonical referrer feed source.
- Reuse one source/projection across streetside, generic referrer, influencer,
  and partner views.

### 3. Referral ownership must be immutable enough to trust
### 3. Ownership Is Mostly Anchored, But Resolution Is Incomplete

If reservations are not anchored to a referrer actor at creation, downstream
commission logic becomes guesswork.
The reservation route already writes `ReferralActor` and `AttributionSession`
at booking time, with a `DIRECT` fallback.
